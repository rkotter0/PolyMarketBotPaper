**Verdict: $329,100 (19.5% off) MATCHES the market — a fair, well-negotiated price, not a steal.** ⚠ Cross-shop flag: loaded **2026 G23 Paragons with the same ZZ8S** are advertised at **$389,000** ([Ugly John's](https://www.uglyjohns.com/New-Inventory-2026-Nautique-Boat-Super-Air-Nautique-G23-Paragon-Afton-17644039)) and **$365,375** ([American Boat Center](https://americanboatcenter.com/products/2026-nautique-g23-paragon)) — before negotiation. **Dealer claims checked:** ⚠ "Bow thruster standard" is **false twice over** — Nautique's Integrated Steering Assist is a *stern*-mounted thruster and it's an **option** on the standard G23 (standard only on Paragon). It IS on your build sheet, so you're covered, but the framing was wrong. ⚠ "Larger surf tabs": the Surf System/WAVEPLATE is **standard equipment** on every G23 — don't credit it as a dealer concession. Get **freight/prep confirmed in writing** ($3–8K exposure if excluded).

## 3. The "25% effective discount" claim

⚠ **Reject it.** Your discount is 19.5% off the *current* sticker — that's the number. Comparing to a hypothetical MY2027 MSRP is marketing math: you could equally "save 25%" next year by negotiating 19% off the higher sticker. No public evidence of a 7% MY2027 increase exists (2027s are listed "Call for Price"); recent non-redesign years ran ~4–6%/yr. The claim isn't absurd — Nautique raises prices every year — but it's not a discount you receive.

## 4. Net cost to switch — Idaho 6% tax, both scenarios

Idaho: 6% sales tax; **trade-in credit confirmed** (tax on the difference; dealer sales only, trade must be documented at time of sale) — [Idaho STC watercraft guide](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/transportation/watercraft/dealer-sales/).

| | **Trade-in @ $112K** | **Private sale @ $130K** |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $329,100 | $329,100 |
| Taxable amount | $329,100 − $112,000 = $217,100 | $329,100 |
| Idaho 6% tax | **$13,026** | **$19,746** |
| Cash out | $342,126 | $348,846 |
| Less boat proceeds | −$112,000 | −$130,000 |
| **Net cost to switch** | **$230,126** | **$218,846** |

Trade-in tax savings = 6% of trade value = **$6,720**. But the private-sale premium ($18K) exceeds it: **private sale wins by ~$11,280** at these values. Rule: **trade-in only wins if your private price nets below 1.06 × trade offer = ~$118,700.** If you'd actually clear $138K private, net cost drops to $210,846.

## 5. Four-year cost: switch vs. keep (2026 → 2030)

- **New boat from $329.1K basis:** 4-year-old G23s (2022s) ask $159–178K vs ~$250–280K loaded-new → ~55–63% sold-value retention → 2030 value **$185–205K** → depreciation **$125–145K**, plus $19.7K tax sunk.
- **Keep the 2020:** ~$130K today → ~$95–105K in 2030 (9–10 yr comps: 2016–17 G23s ask $85–135K, clustering $90–120K) → depreciation **$25–35K**.
- **Incremental cost of switching ≈ $115K depreciation delta + $19.7K tax + ~$47K opportunity cost (5%/yr on $218.8K, 4 yrs) ≈ $165–180K, or ~$40–45K/year** to run a new 630hp boat instead of the 2020. That's the honest price of the upgrade — the discount doesn't change it.

## 6. Non-Paragon resale exit

The standard G23 depreciates at a similar *percentage* rate to the Paragon (Paragons actually fell hard early: 2022–23 Paragons ask $225–270K off ~$362K MSRP). The real exit risk is different: ⚠ **the 2025 full G-Series redesign caps values of all pre-2025 hulls** — ~130 leftover *new* old-gen G23s are being cleared in the high-$100Ks/low-$200Ks, directly compressing 2020–2024 used values. This argues for selling your 2020 **sooner rather than later**, and slightly favors the deal: the 2026 you'd buy is the new-generation hull. Residual caveat: at resale your loaded non-Paragon will sit near lightly-optioned Paragon money, which can lengthen time-to-sell.

## VERDICT: **GO — moderate-to-high confidence (~75%)**

$329,100 is a market-consistent, well-negotiated price for a genuinely ~$409K build; the buy-now path beats waiting by ~$50–125K in 2029 dollars; Idaho's trade-in credit is real but private sale still nets ~$11K more at realistic values; and the soft used market argues for exiting the 2020 before pre-2025 hulls compress further.

**Where I disagree with your prior work:** (1) Your 2020 is more likely a **$125–135K** private-sale boat than $135–140K — plan proceeds around $130K and treat anything more as upside; the prior $115–130K estimate was nearer the truth than your figure. (2) The "25% effective discount" framing is invalid — you're getting 19.5%, which is *good but ordinary* in this market. (3) The bow-thruster and surf-tab "extras" claims were dealer puffery (one optional-and-stern-mounted, one standard equipment). **Where I agree:** GO at $329,100 *conditional on the power problem meaningfully limiting current use* — the switch costs ~$40–45K/yr in real economic terms, and that's justified by utility, not by the deal being a bargain. **Before signing:** get freight/prep included in writing, confirm the Integrated Steering Assist and trailer are line items on the build sheet, and price a Paragon ($365–389K asks) for $30–50K more given resale positioning.
